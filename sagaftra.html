Case study · Member experience

Making residuals
easier to understand,
track, and trust.

Residuals are an important part of how actors get paid. But for many SAG-AFTRA members, understanding where that money was in the process could be confusing, frustrating, and unnecessarily opaque. I led a team across research, UX, and UI design to rethink the residuals tracking experience from the member’s point of view.

Client
SAG-AFTRA
Year
2025
Scope
Research · UX · UI Design
Research
30 member interviews

About this work: I led this in a director role at a previous agency, before founding Artifact Digital. The problem, the decisions and the outcomes are described as they happened.

Make it easier for actors to understand what they were owed, where their payment was, and what was happening next.

The challenge

The problem wasn’t only the interface.

The existing residuals tracker had usability issues, but the deeper challenge was that the product did not fully reflect how residual payments actually moved through SAG-AFTRA’s internal process. There were important internal steps, statuses, and ways of working that members couldn’t see. That created uncertainty.

An actor might know that a residual existed but still not understand any of the following.

  • Where it was in the process
  • Whether action was required
  • Why it had not been paid
  • What SAG-AFTRA was doing with it
  • What would happen next
  • How long the process might take

The lack of visibility made an already complicated subject feel even harder to understand. Before changing the interface, we needed to understand the entire system behind it.

Research

Start with the people actually waiting for the money.

I led the research effort with SAG-AFTRA members to understand how actors were using the residuals tracker and where the experience was failing them. We created structured testing and interview scripts and spoke with 30 actors who were SAG-AFTRA members.

We wanted to understand more than whether someone could click through the product.

  • How members thought about residuals
  • What information they looked for first
  • Where they became confused
  • What terminology made sense
  • What they believed different statuses meant
  • What they did when they couldn’t find an answer
  • Which information mattered most
  • Where the experience created unnecessary effort
  • What would give them greater confidence

The research gave us a much clearer picture of what members actually needed from the product.

The hidden journey

Members were only seeing part of the process.

One of the most important discoveries was that SAG-AFTRA’s internal residuals workflow contained meaningful steps that were not visible to members. Internally, the organization understood where a payment was and what needed to happen next. The member often didn’t.

That gap created a significant experience problem. What felt like an unexplained delay to an actor might actually be a normal step in the residuals process. The opportunity was to bring more of that internal knowledge into the customer-facing experience.

Instead of forcing members to wonder

“Where is my money?”

We could begin answering

“Here’s exactly where it is.”

Creating the hierarchy

Not every piece of information is equally important.

Our research helped us establish a clearer hierarchy of what members needed to see, which gave us the foundation for simplifying the experience.

  • Most important information
  • Secondary information
  • Required actions
  • Payment status
  • Residual details
  • Process milestones
  • Supporting information
  • Less frequent functionality

Rather than presenting everything with equal weight, we could design around the questions members were actually trying to answer.

  • How much is it?
  • What is it for?
  • Where is it?
  • Do I need to do anything?
  • What happens next?

That hierarchy became the backbone of the redesigned experience.

Simplifying the experience

Fewer clicks. Clearer answers.

One of the primary UX goals was reducing the amount of effort required to understand a residual.

  • Navigation
  • Search
  • Filtering
  • Information architecture
  • Status visibility
  • Detail views
  • Interaction patterns
  • Feature priority

We reduced unnecessary steps and brought the most important information closer to the surface. Instead of making members dig through multiple screens to piece together an answer, the experience was designed to provide the right information earlier.

  • Less navigation.
  • Less interpretation.
  • More clarity.

Designing for transparency

Show the process, not just the result.

A major part of the redesign was bringing SAG-AFTRA’s internal ways of working into the member experience. We wanted members to see more than Paid or Not Paid, so we designed an experience where the status of a residual could communicate more of the actual journey.

The specific language and states needed to reflect SAG-AFTRA’s actual processes, but the principle was consistent: give the member visibility into what’s happening behind the scenes.

  • Reduces uncertainty for the member
  • Explains delays that are actually normal steps
  • Reduces calls asking only for a status update
  • Builds confidence in the process itself

Residual identified → Processing → Additional information required → Payment being prepared → Payment issued

From research to features

Let member needs define the product.

The interviews weren’t just validation for a design we had already decided to make. They directly influenced the hierarchy and feature set. From the research, we identified which capabilities needed to be immediately available and which could remain secondary.

That allowed the product to prioritize the real jobs members were trying to accomplish rather than organizing the interface around internal systems or organizational structure.

Less about how SAG-AFTRA stores residual information. More about how an actor needs to understand it.

Experience design

Turn a complicated process into something understandable.

Residuals can be inherently complicated. The UX didn’t need to add another layer of complexity. My team translated the research and process understanding into a full set of design outputs.

  • Information architecture
  • User flows
  • Feature hierarchy
  • Wireframes
  • Status systems
  • Interaction patterns
  • UI design
  • Testing prototypes

The goal wasn’t to hide important complexity. It was to organize complexity around the member’s mental model. The interface needed to help an actor understand the situation without requiring them to understand SAG-AFTRA’s internal organization first.

Leading across research, UX & UI

Keep the evidence connected to the design.

I led the team across the full design process. That meant making sure the research didn’t become a report that disappeared once design started.

  • Research findings informed our feature hierarchy.
  • The hierarchy informed our flows.
  • The flows informed our wireframes.
  • The wireframes informed the interface.
  • And the interface stayed grounded in what members told us.
  1. Research
  2. Prioritize
  3. Simplify
  4. Design
  5. Validate

The result

A residuals experience built around member understanding.

The redesign created a clearer and more transparent way for SAG-AFTRA members to understand their residuals.

  • We reduced unnecessary interaction.
  • We established a stronger information hierarchy.
  • We prioritized the features members actually needed.
  • We brought internal process into the member experience.

The work shifted the experience from simply tracking residual records toward something much more useful: helping members understand the status of their compensation.

The bigger idea

Transparency is a UX feature.

The most interesting lesson from this work wasn’t about navigation or wireframes. It was about trust. When people are waiting for money they’ve earned, silence creates anxiety. The organization may know exactly what’s happening internally, but if the customer can’t see it, that knowledge doesn’t help them.

Our opportunity was to expose the right parts of that process. Not every internal detail. Just enough to help the member understand.

  1. We know about your residual.
  2. Here’s where it is.
  3. Here’s what’s happening.
  4. Here’s what comes next.

That’s what made the work meaningful. We weren’t simply reducing clicks. We were reducing uncertainty.
