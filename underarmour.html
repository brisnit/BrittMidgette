Case study · Future retail

Designing the future
of the athlete
experience.

Under Armour wasn’t simply thinking about the future of retail. The bigger question was what a store could actually do for an athlete. I worked on the experience strategy and concept development for a new vision of Under Armour retail, bringing together physical space, digital technology, performance data, elite training expertise, community, and commerce.

Client
Under Armour
Year
2018
Scope
Experience Strategy · Concept Development
Channels
Physical retail · Connected fitness

About this work: I led this in a director role at a previous agency, before founding Artifact Digital. The problem, the decisions and the outcomes are described as they happened.

What could a store do for an athlete?

The vision

What if the store could make you a better athlete?

Under Armour had something most retailers didn’t. It wasn’t just a product company. It had athletes, trainers, performance expertise, connected fitness technology, and an enormous amount of data about how people move and train. The opportunity was to bring those things together.

I explored how a physical Under Armour environment could become an intelligent experience that knew more about the athlete, responded to them, and helped them improve.

Instead of thinking

How do we sell more products in the store?

The question became

How do we create more value for the athlete?

If we could do that, commerce could become a natural part of the relationship.

Immersion & strategy

Understand the athlete. Understand the brand. Then imagine what’s possible.

The work began by immersing myself in Under Armour’s world. I looked across the brand, its products, connected fitness ecosystem, athlete relationships, training expertise, retail experience, and the role technology could play inside a physical environment.

The goal wasn’t to add screens to a store. It was to identify experiences that could only exist because of what Under Armour already had. That led to a much broader vision for a Human Performance Center, built around three interconnected ideas.

01

Innovation

Use technology and data to make the invisible parts of athletic performance visible.

02

Sport

Create experiences that let everyday athletes feel closer to the intensity, emotion, and mindset of elite competition.

03

Personalization

Use data and human expertise to make products, training, recovery, and recommendations more relevant to the individual athlete.

From vision to concepts

Turn an ambitious idea into experiences people can actually use.

From that strategy, I explored a larger ecosystem of potential experiences, investigating how Under Armour could combine what it already had.

  • Performance data
  • Training
  • Recovery
  • Connected fitness
  • Community
  • Product
  • Personalization
  • Physical retail
  • Digital interaction

The goal was to find ideas that felt distinctly Under Armour rather than technology that could have been dropped into any retail store. Two pilot experiences emerged as particularly strong expressions of that vision. One focused on the individual athlete. The other focused on the community of athletes around them.

Pilot one · Armour Bar

Human optimization powered by Under Armour.

Armour Bar was conceived as a human optimization station, giving everyday athletes access to some of the same thinking, expertise, training methods, and recovery knowledge available to Under Armour’s elite athletes.

Instead of walking into the store and only asking “what should I buy?”, an athlete could begin asking “how can I get better?” The experience could combine information the athlete provided with performance data and expert assessment to create recommendations tailored to that individual.

  • Performance
  • Training
  • Recovery
  • Goals
  • Equipment
  • Product recommendations

Technology provided the intelligence. Human expertise provided the meaning.

Technology + expertise

Data is only valuable when it helps someone make a better decision.

I didn’t want Armour Bar to become another dashboard filled with fitness metrics. The experience needed to translate information into action.

  • What should I work on?
  • Where could I improve?
  • How should I recover?
  • What equipment makes sense for the way I train?
  • What should I do next?

Under Armour’s elite training expertise became the layer that transformed raw information into something useful. That created an experience where digital technology could actually make the human interaction better rather than replacing it.

Pilot two · Map My Run

Turn the world’s greatest cities into your running course.

The second pilot explored a completely different side of the Under Armour ecosystem: running and community. Map My Run let runners discover personalized courses and connect their physical run to a larger community experience.

The idea was to transform the city itself into part of Under Armour’s digital experience. Rather than simply tracking distance and pace, runners could discover routes designed around what actually mattered to them.

  • Distance
  • Ability
  • Training goals
  • Location
  • Landmarks
  • Community activity

Start at home

Baltimore.

The first city was an obvious choice. Baltimore is the home of Under Armour. We began there, exploring how runners could experience the city through personalized routes while connecting their individual run to the broader Under Armour running community.

  • Where should I run?
  • How far should I go?
  • What route fits my goals?
  • What are other runners doing around me?

Instead of technology merely recording the run after it happened, the experience could become part of deciding where the adventure should go next.

  • The route
  • The community

A platform for the world’s cities

Baltimore was the beginning.

The larger vision was something that could expand far beyond a single location. Imagine opening Map My Run while visiting any of them.

  • New York
  • Chicago
  • Los Angeles
  • London
  • Paris
  • Tokyo

Instead of searching the internet for somewhere to run, Under Armour could give you a course personalized to you.

  • A five-mile training run.
  • A scenic recovery run.
  • A challenging route.
  • A course through iconic landmarks.
  • A route other athletes love.
Your relationship with Under Armour could travel with you.

Designing an ecosystem

Physical and digital didn’t need to be separate experiences.

Armour Bar and Map My Run represented two sides of the same larger idea.

Armour Bar

  • Understand me
  • Help me improve
  • Give me access to expertise

Map My Run

  • Get me moving
  • Connect me to my environment
  • Connect me to other athletes

Together they demonstrated what could happen when Under Armour stopped thinking about digital, retail, product, training, and community as separate channels. They could become one athlete experience.

The bigger idea

Retail doesn’t have to begin and end with a transaction.

This work changed the question from “how do we create a better Under Armour store?” to “how can Under Armour create a better athlete?” That distinction opened up an entirely different set of possibilities.

  • A store could become a place to learn.
  • Performance data could become guidance.
  • Elite expertise could become accessible.
  • A running app could become a way to experience a city.
  • And the relationship could continue long after someone walked out the door.

For me, that’s what made the project exciting. I wasn’t designing technology to sit inside a store. I was exploring how technology, data, physical space, and human expertise could work together to extend what the Under Armour brand could actually do for an athlete.
